Why the median is not the district
Half of Layyah's schools are above 66.7%; the district as a whole is at 33.9%. Diagnosis · strengths

Four things Layyah is demonstrably doing well

The district is not failing across the board — these are the foundations a recovery plan should build on.
1 · Elementary enrollment is on track

M-EE is at 62.5% and W-EE at 51.5%; together the elementary wings need only 3,627 more students — a 2.3% lift. Karor Lalisan's elementary wings are effectively at target.

159,979 students · 34% of the district gap
2 · Most schools are at or above target

509 schools (42%) have met their target and the median school sits at 66.7%. The habit of hitting a school-level target exists; it needs to be extended to the large schools.

Median school achievement 66.7%
3 · Twenty markaz have already delivered

Eighteen of the twenty markaz at or above target are in Karor Lalisan, with enrollment of 1,300–2,200 students each — proof that markaz-scale delivery is possible, not just school-scale.

20 of 100 markaz at 100%+
4 · Large secondary schools can move

GHS MUSLIM CHOWK AZAM added 59 students, GHSS CHAK NO. 93/ML 50 and GHSS CHAK NO. 139-A/TDA LAYYAH 44 — all schools of 700–1,400 students. The secondary slide is a management problem, not a demographic one.

Best eight large schools added 397 students
What this means
The district does not need a new strategy. It needs to apply the practices of its elementary and Karor systems to its large secondary schools — and to hold markaz accountable for the 80 clusters that are not yet at target.
