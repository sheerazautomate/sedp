# District Layyah — presenter notes

Companion to `layyah/index.html` (30 slides). Figures are from the SIS snapshot of
**22 Oct 2026, 06:26 AM PKT** and the Grades baseline/target export of
**14 Oct 2026, 05:50 PM PKT**. Regenerate the deck with
`python3 scripts/build_layyah_deck.py`.

---

## 1 · Cover (1 min)
Open with the headline: **266,817 students enrolled, 33.9% of the way to a 277,455 target.**
Set expectations — this is not a district-wide failure story. It is a story about where the
10,638 missing students actually sit.

## 2 · Executive summary (3 min)
Four numbers, in this order:
1. **33.9%** of the target increment — 5,451 added, 10,638 still needed.
2. **19.9 pp** gender gap in achievement (boys 43.9%, girls 24.0%); girls are 59% of the remaining need.
3. **66%** of the gap sits in the 200 secondary schools, which teach 40% of students.
4. **30%** of the gap sits in 20 schools — closing them alone lifts the district to 53.6%.
Land the diagnosis: elementary is working, large secondary schools are not.

## 3 · Scope & method (2 min)
Say plainly what is being compared: current enrollment from SIS, baseline and target from Grades.
Achievement = (current − baseline) ÷ (target − baseline), uncapped, computed on summed school
values — never an average of school percentages.
Flag early: **799 schools have a target only 1–10 students above baseline**, so school-level
percentages swing wildly (−800% to +3,000%). Use them as flags, not league tables.

## 4 · District scorecard (2 min)
Current 266,817 (boys 133,785 · girls 133,010); baseline 261,366; target 277,455.
Added 5,451 (+2.1%); still required 10,638 (+4.0% on current).
Every 1,000 students recovered = **6.2 percentage points**.

## 5 · Distance to target (2 min)
Walk the bridge left to right: baseline → current → target.
Two-thirds of the required growth is outstanding. Average per school is only **8.7 students** —
the problem is concentration, not the size of the ask.
Gender split of the remaining need: boys 4,421, girls 6,239.

## 6 · Gender (3 min)
Girls started **720 ahead** of boys at baseline and are now **775 behind**.
The target asks girls to add 8,206; they have added 1,967. Boys added 3,462.
If girls had matched the boys' rate, the district would be at **43.9%** instead of 33.9% —
a 10-point difference with no change in boys' performance.

## 10 · School health (2 min)

### Answers to likely questions

**“Why is the district at 33.9% when the median school is 66.7%?”**
Because achievement is enrollment-weighted. Half the schools are small and doing well; the
thousands of students in large secondary schools are not.

**“Is the target realistic?”**
The full 16,089-student increment is large, but 55 schools carry most of it. Closing the top 20
alone moves the district to 53.6%.

**“Why do some schools show −300% or +3,000%?”**
Their target is only a few students above baseline, so each student is worth tens of points.
That is why this deck ranks by students needed, not percentage.

**“Why do markaz counts differ from the dashboard's 101?”**
The source lists three “Secondary Wing” rows as markaz; these are wing aggregates, not AEO
clusters, so they are excluded here (100 real markaz).

**“Is any of this a data problem?”**
Partly: 99 schools record no girls and 66 no boys, and 2 record no enrollment. Workstream 5
exists to settle that before the next target cycle.
